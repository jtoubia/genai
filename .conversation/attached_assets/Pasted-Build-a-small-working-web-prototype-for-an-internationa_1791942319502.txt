Build a small working web prototype for an international cooking assistant.

PRODUCT PURPOSE
Help a young adult who wants to cook an unfamiliar international dish understand what ingredients they need and where to look for specialty items.

Build one complete flow:
Choose a dish and basic preferences → click “Create my shopping plan” → receive an understandable ingredient and sourcing plan.

This is a course prototype. Use prepared responses and sample data to simulate future AI behavior. No live AI integration, API keys, database, or backend is required.

STARTING SCREEN
Show the heading “From dish idea to shopping plan.”
Add a short explanation: “Choose a dish to discover what you need, understand unfamiliar ingredients, and find places to look for them.”

Provide:
1. A required dish dropdown with three supported examples:
   - Thai green curry
   - Chicken biryani
   - Pad kra pao
2. A servings selector: 2 or 4.
3. A dietary preference selector: No restriction or Dairy-free.
4. A button labeled “Create my shopping plan.”

Show the location as “South Bend, Indiana — demo location.” Do not provide a location input that the prototype cannot actually use.

Include a clearly visible note: “Prototype: prepared recommendations and illustrative store options; no live AI or inventory checks.”

RESULT
After submission, display a shopping plan matching the selected dish, servings, and dietary preference. Include:

1. Ingredients to buy
- Separate common supermarket ingredients from specialty ingredients.
- Include quantities for the selected servings.
- Use a consistent prepared recipe for each dish; double quantities when servings change from 2 to 4.

2. Understand the specialty ingredients
- Briefly explain each unfamiliar ingredient and its role in the dish.
- Provide substitution guidance for selected specialty ingredients.
- Explain how each substitute changes the flavor or character of the dish.
- Clearly distinguish the traditional ingredient from an approximation.
- If there is no close substitute, say so.

3. Where to look
- Group ingredients into a practical shopping plan: common items at a regular supermarket and specialty items at an appropriate international grocery.
- Use clearly labeled fictional sample stores, such as “Demo Asian Grocery” and “Demo Neighborhood Supermarket.”
- Match store suggestions to the relevant ingredients.
- Do not invent real addresses, distances, hours, or confirmed stock.
- Label these options “Illustrative store option — check availability before visiting.”

DIETARY BEHAVIOR
Make the dietary preference affect the plan where relevant:
- For dairy-free chicken biryani, replace dairy yogurt and ghee with a prepared alternative and explain the expected difference.
- For dishes whose prepared recipes already contain no dairy, explicitly state that no dairy substitution is needed.
- Remind users to check packaged ingredient labels; do not claim allergy safety.

END-TO-END BEHAVIOR
- Keep the submitted selections visible with the result.
- Include an “Edit my choices” button so users can change inputs and generate another plan.
- Validate the required dish selection with a clear message.
- Ensure every button and selector works.
- Do not display unrelated results or pretend to support dishes beyond the three examples.
- The main flow must work from beginning to end without dead ends.

DESIGN
Use a simple, readable, mobile-friendly layout with clear labels and short sections. Focus on usability. No elaborate landing page, generated images, or animations are necessary.

SCOPE
Do not add accounts, payments, restaurant discovery, delivery, maps, live inventory, saved plans, dashboards, chat, or unsupported dietary options.

IMPLEMENTATION AND DOCUMENTATION
Use a lightweight frontend and keep the prepared dish data easy to edit.

Create a README explaining:
- The product’s purpose and intended user.
- How to run and use the prototype.
- What works: choosing supported dishes, changing servings and dietary preferences, viewing a matching plan, and editing choices.
- What is simulated: AI interpretation, ingredient recommendations, substitutions, and local store matching.
- What future AI would do: interpret open-ended cooking requests and produce contextual ingredient explanations and substitution guidance.
- Current limitations: three prepared dishes, two serving sizes, one dietary restriction, a fixed demo location, and fictional store options.

Verify these three flows before finishing:
1. Thai green curry for 2 with no restriction displays common and specialty ingredients and sourcing guidance.
2. Chicken biryani for 4 with Dairy-free selected displays scaled quantities and dairy substitutions.
3. Pad kra pao explains Thai holy basil, the tradeoff of substituting Thai sweet basil, and uncertain store availability.

Report any remaining issues honestly. Do not claim that these checks constitute real user testing.
